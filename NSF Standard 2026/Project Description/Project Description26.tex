\noindent\textbf{Overview.} Castelnuovo--Mumford regularity and graded Betti numbers are standard tools linking the geometry of a projective variety to the algebra of its equations. This proposal extends parts of this theory beyond the standard graded setting: to nonstandard $\ZZ$-gradings, which arise from stacky curves, and for $\ZZ^r$-gradings, which arise from toric varieties. After prior support (\S\ref{sec:prior-work}), we develop a Green's-conjecture-type theory of syzygies of stacky canonical curves (\S\ref{sec:syzygies-on-stacks}); structural results and geometric bounds for multigraded regularity (\S\ref{sec:multigraded-regularity}); a uniform regularity theory on the Cox category of a toric variety (\S\ref{sec:uniform-homological-algebra-on-toric}); and open-source software (\S\ref{sec:comp}), with broader impacts in \S\ref{sec:broader-impacts}.

\subsection{Eisenbud-Goto on Products of Projective Space}\label{subsec:prior:homological-algebra-on-toric}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The Castelnuovo--Mumford regularity of a projective variety $X\subset \PP^{r}$ measures the complexity of $X$ through the vanishing of its cohomology. Its centrality rests on the fact that a single integer controls cohomology vanishing, the degrees of generators and syzygies, and the eventual linearity of resolutions.

\begin{theorem}\cite{eisenbudGoto84}\label{thm:eisenbud-goto}
Let $\cF$ be a coherent sheaf on $\PP^{n}$ and $M=\bigoplus_{e\in\ZZ} H^0(\PP^{n},\cF(e))$ the corresponding section module. The following are equivalent: 
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is $d$-regular in the sense of \cite{mumford65};
\item $\beta_{p,q}(M)=0$ for all $p\geq0$ and $q>d+p$; and
\item $M_{\geq d}$ has a linear resolution in the sense of Eisenbud-Goto.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}


Nearly every use of regularity in the literature -- regularity of powers of ideals \cite{CHT99,Kodiyalam00}, the Eisenbud--Goto conjecture \cite{MP18}, the construction and boundedness of Hilbert schemes \cite{grothendieck61,gotzmann78}, Gr\"obner basis complexity \cite{bayerStillman87}, etc. -- passes through this equivalence in a crucial way.

A toric variety $X$ is a well-behaved compactification of a torus and, under mild hypotheses, a quotient $X=(\CC^{n}\setminus \VV(B))/(\CC^{\times})^r$, where $B$ is the \emph{irrelevant ideal} of $X$; for $X = \PP^n$, this is $B=\langle x_0,\ldots,x_n\rangle$. Cox's theorem \cite{cox95} identifies coherent sheaves on $X$ with $B$-saturated $\ZZ^r$-graded modules over the \emph{Cox ring} $S=\CC[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, translating the geometry of $X$ into multigraded commutative algebra. This dictionary is far subtler than in the standard graded case, and its subtleties drive much current work: virtual resolutions and resolutions of the diagonal \cite{BES20, HHL24, brownErman24-1, faveroHuang23}, generalizations of the BGG correspondence \cite{EES15,brownErmanTate}, and multigraded local cohomology \cite{botbolChardin17, chardinNemati20}.

Extending regularity beyond projective space has a long history. Early developments include Benson's treatment of nonstandard $\ZZ$-graded rings \cite{benson04}, later developed by Symonds in his groundbreaking work on invariant theory \cite{symonds10,symonds11}, and Hoffman and Wang's theory for products of two projective spaces \cite{hoffmanWang04}. In the toric setting, Maclagan and Smith gave a cohomological definition of multigraded regularity \cite{maclaganSmith04} that subsumes the weighted and biprojective theories and has become the standard framework for extending the classical theory to toric varieties.

%n a different direction, Pareschi and Popa introduced Mukai regularity on abelian varieties \cite{pareschiPopa03}. 
Throughout, $X$ denotes a smooth projective toric variety, so that $\Pic(X)\cong \ZZ^r$ and the nef cone $\Nef(X)_{\RR} \subset \Pic(X)_{\RR}$ is full-dimensional. We write $\Nef(X)\coloneqq \Nef(X)_{\RR} \cap \Pic(X)$ for the nef monoid and $B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}$ for its unique minimal generators. For $\vv \in \ZZ^r$, set $|\vv|\coloneqq v_1+\cdots+v_r$.

\begin{defn}\cite[Def. 6.6]{maclaganSmith04}\label{def:mg-reg}
A coherent sheaf $\cF$ on $X$ is \emph{$\dd$-regular}, for $\dd\in\ZZ^r$, if for all $i>0$:
\[
H^i(X, \cF(\ee))=0 \qquad \text{for all } \qquad \ee \in L_{i}(\dd) \coloneqq \bigcup_{\substack{\vv\in \NN^s, |\vv|=i}} \left(\dd-\sum_{j=1}^{s}v_{j}B_j + \Nef(X)\right)
\]
The \emph{multigraded regularity} of $\cF$ is $\reg(\cF)\coloneqq \{\dd\in \ZZ^r \;\; | \;\; \text{$\cF$ is $\dd$-regular}\}$. 
\end{defn}

Multigraded regularity has become a central tool in the study of uniform bounds and Hilbert schemes \cite{maclaganSmith05}, syzygies and local cohomology \cite{sidmanVanTuylWang06,botbolChardin17}, virtual resolutions \cite{BES20,bruceHellerSayrafi26}, and asymptotic regularity \cite{bruceHellerSayrafiSeceleanu25}. Yet for more than fifteen years after its introduction, a foundational question remained open: \emph{Is there a multigraded Eisenbud--Goto theorem?} That is, can multigraded regularity be characterized by free resolutions and truncations, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:eisenbud-goto}? Direct generalizations fail even for products of projective spaces. Answering a question of Maclagan--Smith, further studied by Botbol--Chardin \cite{botbolChardin17}, the PI and coauthors showed that multigraded Betti numbers do not determine regularity \cite[Example~5.1]{bruceHellerSayrafi26}, ruling out an analogue of condition~(2). Likewise, a $\dd$-regular module $M$ need not have $M_{\geq \dd}$ with a linear resolution, so condition~(3) must also be reformulated.

A major advance came with Berkesch, Erman, and Smith's characterization of multigraded regularity on products of projective spaces via virtual resolutions with controlled degrees \cite[Theorem~2.9]{BES20}, a counterpart to condition~(2). Building on these ideas, the PI and coauthors introduced \emph{quasilinear resolutions} and proved a multigraded Eisenbud--Goto theorem \cite{bruceHellerSayrafi26}: if $M$ is a finitely generated module over the Cox ring of a product of projective spaces with $H^0_B(M)=0$, then $M$ is $\dd$-regular if and only if $M_{\geq \dd}$ has a quasilinear resolution generated in degree $\dd$. This gives a precise homological characterization of multigraded regularity through resolutions of truncations.

\subsection{Bounds \& Asymptotic Behavior of Multigraded Regularity of Ideals}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We may place a partial order on $\Pic(X)$ by saying that $\dd\leq\ee$ when $\ee-\dd\in \Nef(X)$. Although nef twists preserve regularity, $\reg(M)$ need not have a finite number of minimal elements with respect to this partial order, i.e., pictorially, the regularity region need not have finitely many corners. In \cite[Example~1.1]{bruceHellerSayrafi22}, the PI, Cranton Heller, and Sayrafi exhibit a cyclic module on a Hirzebruch surface with infinitely many minimal degrees. We show that for faithful modules such pathologies cannot occur. 

\begin{theorem}\cite[Corollary~4.6]{bruceHellerSayrafi22}
Let $M$ be a finitely generated graded $S$-module with $\ann(M)=0$ and $\widetilde M\neq0$. If its homogeneous generators $f_i$ satisfy $\qq\leq\deg f_i$, then $\reg(M)\subseteq\qq+\Nef(X)$. Consequently, $\reg(M)$ has finitely many minimal elements.
\end{theorem}
The proof uses that $\dd$-regularity forces $M_{\geq\dd}$ to be generated in degree $\dd$; a chamber-and-determinant argument turns a violation of the bound into a nonzero annihilator of $M$. It applies to every nonzero homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq S$, though $\reg(S/I)$ may have infinitely many corners.

%The proof uses the fact that $\dd$-regularity forces $M_{\geq\dd}$ to be generated in degree $\dd$. A chamber and determinant argument turns a violation of the bound into a nonzero annihilator of $M$. This applies to every nonzero homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq S$, even though, as the aforementioned example shows, $\reg(S/I)$ need not be finitely generated. 

For ordinary ideal powers in the standard graded setting, Swanson established linear bounds on regularity~\cite{swanson97}, and Cutkosky--Herzog--Trung and Kodiyalam proved eventual linearity~\cite{CHT99,Kodiyalam00}. Combining our bound with a resolution of the Rees algebra gives the first multigraded analog of these results, showing that regularity of powers of ideals translates at a linear rate.

\begin{cor}\cite[Theorem~5.1]{bruceHellerSayrafi22}
Let $I=\langle f_1,\ldots,f_s\rangle\subseteq S$ be a nonzero homogeneous ideal. There exists $\aa\in\Pic(X)$, depending only on $I$, such that for every integer $t>0$ and all $\qq_1,\qq_2\in\Pic(X)$ satisfying $\qq_1\geq\deg f_i\geq\qq_2$ for every $i$,
\[
t\qq_1+\aa+\reg(S)\subseteq\reg(I^t)\subseteq t\qq_2+\Nef(X).
\]
\end{cor}

\section{The Geometry of Syzygies on Stacky Curves}\label{sec:syzygies-on-stacks}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Let $X$ be a projective variety and $L$ a very ample line bundle. Considering the embedding of $X$ into projective space $X \hookrightarrow \PP H^{0}(X,L)\cong \PP^{n}$ defined by $L$, let $S_{X}=S/I_{X}$, where $S=\CC[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]$ is the standard graded polynomial ring and $I_{X}$ is the homogeneous defining ideal of $X$. The minimal graded free resolution of $S_{X}$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep = 3em]
0 & \lar{} S_{X} & \arrow[l,"\epsilon" above]  F_{0} & \arrow[l,"d_{1}" above] \cdots &  & \cdots & \arrow[l,"d_{k-1}" above]  F_{k-1} & \arrow[l,"d_{k}" above] F_{k} & \lar \cdots
\end{tikzcd}
\end{center}
has terms $F_p=\bigoplus_q S(-q)^{\beta_{p,q}(X;L)}$, where $S(-q)_d=S_{d-q}$. The \emph{graded Betti numbers (or syzygies)} $\beta_{p,q}(X;L)$ depend on the extrinsic embedding, not just on $X$; an overarching theme in the geometry of syzygies is understanding how they nonetheless capture the intrinsic geometry of $X$.

Forty years after Green's work, this connection is best understood for canonical curves ($L=K_X$), where Green's conjecture \cite{green84-I} predicts that the Clifford index alone governs these Betti numbers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:greens}
\beta_{p,p+2}(X;K_X)=0 \quad \quad \text{if and only if} \quad \quad p<\cliff(X).
\end{equation}
Here $\cliff(X)\coloneqq \min \{\deg(L) - 2 \dim H^{0}(X,L)+2\}$, over line bundles with $\deg(L) \leq g-1$ and $\dim H^0(X,L) \geq 2$. Breakthrough work of Voisin proved this for general curves of every genus \cite{voisin02,voisin05}; new proofs \cite{aprodu19,kemeny21} and many refinements followed \cite{farkasMustataPopa03,chiodoEFS13,farkasKemeny16,farkasKemeny17,deopurkar18,kemeny24}.

Breakthrough work of Voisin proved this for general curves of every genus \cite{voisin02,voisin05}, and in recent years new proofs have been found \cite{aprodu19,kemeny21}. Substantial work has gone into finding refinements and extensions of Green's Conjecture \cite{farkasMustataPopa03,chiodoEFS13,farkasKemeny16,farkasKemeny17,deopurkar18,kemeny24}.

Stacky curves and their section rings connect commutative algebra, geometry, and number theory \cite{abramovichVistoli02,cadman07,AGV08,voightZurieckBrown22}. Algebraically, they arise from two-dimensional quotient singularities, and their section rings fit the Demazure--Pinkham description of normal graded rings via ample $\QQ$-divisors on curves \cite{pinkham77,demazure88,watanabe81}. Geometrically, Weierstrass models, hyperelliptic canonical rings, and log canonical rings all require generators of unequal degrees, placing questions about equations and syzygies in weighted projective geometry. Arithmetically, canonical rings of stacky modular and Shimura curves encode modular and automorphic forms \cite{voightZurieckBrown22}, and the stack structure of modular curves drives a growing literature on rational points \cite{bhargavaPoonen20,nasserdenXiao20,kobinZB25}. The classical theory of Noether, Petri, Mumford, and Green is a theory of standard graded rings, and it breaks down as soon as the divisor fails to be very ample. Already for a point $O$ on an elliptic curve $E$, the section ring $\bigoplus_n H^0(E,nO)$ needs generators in degrees $1,2,3$, and its presentation is the Weierstrass equation $y^2=x^3+axz^4+bz^6$, a sextic in $\PP(1,2,3)$; the canonical ring of a hyperelliptic curve needs generators in degree $2$, with the hyperelliptic equation a relation in degree $4$. Stacky curves are the systematic setting for this phenomenon: by Demazure--Pinkham \cite{pinkham77,demazure88,watanabe81}, every two-dimensional normal graded domain is $\bigoplus_n H^0(C,\lfloor nD\rfloor)$ for an ample $\QQ$-divisor $D$ on a curve $C$, i.e., a section ring on a stacky curve whose stacky points record the fractional part of $D$. \subsection{Weighted Canonical Rings}\label{subsec:weighted-canonical}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

A \defi{stacky curve} $\cX$ is a smooth proper geometrically connected Deligne--Mumford stack of dimension one with a dense open subscheme. Concretely, we may think of $\cX$ as a curve $C$ together with finitely many \emph{stacky points} carrying cyclic stabilizers \cite[Lem.~5.3.10]{voightZurieckBrown22}, and the coarse space map $\pi\colon \cX \to C$ forgets these stabilizers. If $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ are the stacky points, $e_i = \#G(x_i)$, $P_i = \pi(x_i)$, and $g = g(C)$, the \emph{signature} of $\cX$ is $(g;e_1,\ldots,e_s)$. Following \cite{voightZurieckBrown22}, the \emph{canonical divisor} on such a stacky curve is defined to be:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
	K_{\cX}=\pi^*K_C+\sum_{i}\left(e_i-1\right) x_i = \pi^*D \qquad \text{where} \qquad D=K_{C}+\sum_{i}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{e_i}\right)P_i
\end{equation}
and its degree is $\deg(K_{\cX}) = 2g-2+\sum_i(1-\tfrac1{e_i})$, which may be a rational number. Thus the genus of $\cX$, defined by $\deg(K_{\cX}) = 2g_{\cX}-2$, is the rational number $g_{\cX} = g+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i(1-\tfrac{1}{e_i})\geq g$. We say $\cX$ is \emph{hyperbolic} when $\deg(K_{\cX})>0$. The \emph{canonical ring} of $\cX$ is
\[
R\left(\cX,K_{\cX}\right) \coloneqq \bigoplus_{d\in \ZZ} H^0\left(\cX, dK_{\cX}\right) \cong \bigoplus_{d\in \ZZ} H^0\left(C, \pi_*(dK_{\cX})\right)  \cong \bigoplus_{d\in \ZZ} H^0\left(C, \lfloor dD \rfloor\right) 
\]
where the \emph{round down} of a $\QQ$-divisor $D=\sum_i a_iP_i$ on $C$ is the honest divisor $\lfloor D\rfloor \coloneqq \sum_i \lfloor a_i\rfloor P_i$, and the last isomorphism follows from  \cite[\S5.4]{voightZurieckBrown22}. When $\cX$ is understood, we write $R$ for $R(\cX, K_{\cX})$. 


This round-down is a source of an essential difference from the classical theory. Since $\lfloor D\rfloor=K_C$, $R_1=H^0(\cX,K_{\cX})\cong H^0(C,K_C)$ has dimension $g(C)$ rather than $g(\cX)$. When stacky points are present, these sections vanish along the ramification divisor $K_{\cX}-\pi^*K_C$, so they neither embed $\cX$ nor generate $R$. Adding higher-degree generators yields an embedding into a weighted projective stack \cite{braggOlssonWebb24}; algebraically, $R$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring with a nonstandard $\ZZ$-grading.

Following Brown and Erman \cite[\S2]{brownErman25}, define the \emph{minimal weighted canonical series} $\cW=\bigoplus_{d\geq1}\cW_d$ by setting $\cW_1=R_1$ and, for $d\geq2$, choosing $\cW_d$ as a complement in $R_d$ to the degree-$d$ part of the subalgebra generated by $\cW_1,\ldots,\cW_{d-1}$. Then $\cW$ embeds $\cX$ into $\cP(\cW)$, and $R\cong S/I_{\cX}$, where $S=\Sym(\cW)$. For $g\geq2$, Voight and Zureick-Brown's stacky Noether--Petri theorem gives sharp degree bounds of $e\coloneqq\max(1,e_1,\ldots,e_s)$ for generators and $2e$ for relations, subject to exceptional cases \cite[Cor.~1.4.2]{voightZurieckBrown22}. These bounds do not specify the minimal generator degrees. The first goal of this project is to close this gap by making the generation bound exact.

Let $\nu_d = \dim \cW_d$; the \emph{weight multiset} of $\cW$ has $\nu_d$ copies of each weight $d$, written $d^{\nu_d}$. Set $N = \sum_d \nu_d$, so $\PP(\cW)=\PP(1^{\nu_1},2^{\nu_2},\ldots)$ has dimension $N-1$, $\sigma = \sum_d d\,\nu_d$, and $c = N-2$.

%Let $\nu_d = \dim \cW_d$, and record $\cW$ by its \emph{weight multiset}, in which each weight $d$ appears $\nu_d$ times (we write $a^m$ for $a$ repeated $m$ times). We also set $N = \sum_d \nu_d$, so that $\PP(\cW)=\PP(1^{\nu_1},2^{\nu_2},\ldots)$ has dimension $N-1$, together with $\sigma = \sum_d d\,\nu_d$ and $c = N-2$.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:weights}
Let $\cX$ be a hyperbolic stacky curve with $g\geq3$. The weight multiset of $\cW$ is $(1^{g},\ (2,3,\ldots,e_1),\ \ldots,\ (2,3,\ldots,e_s))$  with $2^{g-2}$ inserted if and only if $C$ is hyperelliptic.
\end{conjecture}

This conjecture implies that the weight multiset already detects whether $C$ is hyperelliptic, and that the generation degree is exactly $e$. We will prove this by producing a generator $y_{i,j}\in R_j$ of pole order exactly $j-1$ at $P_i$ for $2\leq j\leq e_i$, and then showing these are minimal because $\lfloor d\tfrac{e-1}{e}\rfloor\leq d-1$ forces a product of $k\geq2$ generators of total degree $j$ to have pole order at most $j-k$. That these elements in fact generate is the content of the following conjecture.

Filter $R$ by the order of the poles at the stacky points by letting $F_{\ell}R_d \coloneqq H^0\big(C,dK_C+\sum_i\min\{\ell,b_i(d)\}P_i\big)$ with $b_i(d)=\lfloor d(e_i-1)/e_i\rfloor$. This is a multiplicative exhaustive filtration on $R$, and $F_{0}R = R(C, K_C)$. Let $T_e = \CC[\Gamma_e]$ for the semigroup $\Gamma_e=\{(n,\ell):0\leq\ell\leq\lfloor n\tfrac{e-1}{e}\rfloor\}$. The pole filtration gives a flat Rees degeneration from $R$ to $\operatorname{gr}_F R$. We expect its special fiber to separate the principal parts at the stacky points, with products between distinct positive-pole contributions vanishing and coarse canonical sections acting by evaluation.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:degeneration}
Let $\cX$ be a hyperbolic stacky curve with $g\geq3$. Then $\operatorname{gr}_F R$ is the fiber product of $R(C,K_C)$ and $\prod_iT_{e_i}$ over $\prod_i\CC[z_i]$, where the maps evaluate coarse sections at $P_i$ and kill positive-pole monomials in $T_{e_i}$. In particular, there is an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber}
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_F R
\longrightarrow R(C,K_C)\oplus\bigoplus_iT_{e_i}
\longrightarrow\bigoplus_i\CC[z_i]
\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

Computations, including Figure~\ref{fig:betti}, support this description. I will prove it by identifying the graded pieces and their multiplication through principal parts. Together with pole-order minimality, this would establish the generator formula in Conjecture~\ref{conj:weights}. The resulting long exact sequence in $\Tor$ provides a starting point for computing the special-fiber syzygies and tracking the cancellations that recover the Betti table of $R$.

\subsection{The Shape of Stacky Green's Conjecture}\label{subsec:weighted-canonical}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Classically, the Betti table of a canonical curve has four rows and $g-1$ columns. The top and bottom rows are a single $1$, so the content lies in the two middle rows, which are exchanged by the Gorenstein duality $\beta_{p,q}(C,K_{C})=\beta_{g-2-p,\,g+1-q}(C,K_C)$. Thus Green's conjecture \eqref{eq:greens} does two things at once: (i) it determines the length of the linear strand of every canonical curve, and (ii) together with the Hilbert function, it determines the entire Betti table of a general curve, since for general $C$ the two middle rows do not overlap. Stacky canonical rings remain Gorenstein, but can have far more than four rows.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:duality}
Let $\cX$ be a hyperbolic stacky curve with $g\geq3$. Let $R=S/I$ be a minimal graded presentation of $R$ where $S=\KK[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$ with  total weight $\sigma$, and $c=N-2$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is Gorenstein, with $\beta_{i,j}(R)=\beta_{c-i,\sigma+1-j}(R)$.
\item The weighted regularity, in the sense of \cite{brownErman25}, of $R$ is $3$.
\item Its nonzero Betti entries lie in the row range $0\leq j-i\leq\rho-1$, where $\rho=\sigma-N+4$; both endpoint
rows are attained. Assuming Conjecture~\ref{conj:weights} $g\geq3$, we have that
\[
\rho=\begin{cases}
4+\sum_i\binom{e_i}{2},&C\text{ non-hyperelliptic},\\
g+2+\sum_i\binom{e_i}{2},&C\text{ hyperelliptic}.
\end{cases}
\]
Intervening rows may be empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Towards a Weak Form of Stacky Green's Conjecture}\label{subsec:stacky-green}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The weak form requires Green's $N_p$-conditions for nonstandard gradings. Brown and Erman call $Z\subset\PP(\cW)$ \defi{weighted $N_p$} if it is normally generated and $F_i$ is generated in degree at most $w^{i+1}$ for all $i\leq p$, where $w^j$ is the sum of the $j$ largest weights \cite[Def.~1.2]{brownErman25}; for standard weights this is Green's classical $N_p$. Let $\tau_{\BE}(\cX)$ be the smallest $p\geq1$ for which weighted $N_p$ fails. For non-stacky curves, Brown and Erman prove a weighted $N_p$ theorem: a smooth curve of genus $g$ embedded by a normally generated weighted linear series $\cW$ associated to a line bundle of degree at least $2g+1$, with $\nu_1>g$, satisfies weighted $N_p$ for all $1\leq p\leq \dim\cW-g-2$ \cite[Thm.~1.7]{brownErman25}. The stacky canonical series has $\nu_1=g$, so their methods do not apply directly; their framework is nonetheless the right one, and \cite[Question 7.7]{brownErman25} asks for precisely the canonical analogue. We predict that the threshold is governed by the length of the coarse linear strand, $\ell(C)\coloneqq\max\{r\geq1:\beta_{r,r+1}(C;K_C)\neq0\}$.

\begin{conjecture}[General Weak Stacky Green's Conjecture]\label{con:WSGC}
Let $\cX$ be a hyperbolic stacky curve with $g\geq3$. Then $\tau_{\BE}(\cX)=(N-2)-\ell(C)$.
\end{conjecture}

Green's conjecture predicts $\ell(C)=g-2-\cliff(C)$, so $\tau_{\BE}(\cX)=N-g+\cliff(C)$; combined with Voisin's theorem \cite{voisin02,voisin05}, Conjecture~\ref{con:WSGC} would determine the threshold for a general coarse curve of every genus $g\geq3$, uniformly over all signatures and positions of stacky points. The first ingredient in proving Conjecture~\ref{con:WSGC} is the following comparison result for linear strands.

The strong form aims to recover the full Betti table from $g$, $\cliff(C)$, and the signature. The block decomposition of Conjecture~\ref{conj:degeneration} passes two checks: positive shifts for $T\neq\emptyset$ leave only $T=\emptyset$ in the linear strand, recovering Proposition~\ref{prop:linear-strand}; and the maximal row is $\rho-1$, as required by Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}(b). The simplest stacky case admits a complete description.

\begin{theorem}[One stacky point of order two]\label{thm:order-two}
Let $C$ be non-hyperelliptic of genus $g\geq3$, let $P\in C$, and let $\cX=\sqrt[2]{(C,P)}$. Then for all $p,q$,
\[

-\varepsilon_{p,q},
\]
where $\varepsilon_{p,q}=1$ exactly for
$(p,q)\in\{(1,1),(0,2),(g-1,2),(g-2,3)\}$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Beyond Stacky Canonical Curves}\label{subsec:stacks-further}

The proposed proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:degeneration} never uses that the line bundle is $K_{\cX}$, so we expect it to extend to any divisor on a stacky curve of the form $\cD=\pi^*L\otimes\bigotimes_i\cT_i^{a_i}$, where $L$ is very ample on $C$ and $\cT_i=\cO_{\cX}(x_i)$ as above, so that $\deg\cT_i=1/e_i$ and $\cT_i^{e_i}\cong\pi^*\cO_C(P_i)$. \begin{conjecture}\label{conj:high-degree}
Let $\cL=\pi^*L\otimes\bigotimes_i\cT_i^{a_i}$, with $0<a_i<e_i$, $\gcd(a_i,e_i)=1$, and $\deg L\geq2g+1$. The proposed proof combines Watanabe's description of the canonical module~\cite{watanabe81} with Brown and Erman's bounds on strongly linear strands~\cite{brownErman25}, reducing the first failure to a comparison with the coarse canonical module. The kernel-bundle approach will also address the remaining Betti numbers and extensions beyond this high-degree range. For log canonical rings $R(\cX,K_{\cX}+\Delta)$, the same cohomological framework remains available even when the Gorenstein self-duality used above does not hold. \section{Multigraded Castelnuovo–Mumford Regularity: Bounds and Behavior}\label{sec:multigraded-regularity}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

As discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:prior:homological-algebra-on-toric}, Castelnuovo--Mumford regularity links cohomological vanishing to the degrees of equations and syzygies. The PI's work extends this link to products of projective spaces and proves finite-generation and asymptotic results for regularity on toric varieties~\cite{bruceHellerSayrafi22,bruceHellerSayrafi26}. Basic questions remain open: regularity can have infinitely many corners, it is not determined by the multigraded Betti numbers, and few geometric bounds are known. This project aims to explain the shape of the regularity region through the geometry of its support, bound its corners using syzygies, and bound the regularity of curves using intersection theory. Throughout, $X$, $S$, $B$, and $B_1,\ldots,B_s$ are as in \S\ref{sec:prior-work}, with $n=\dim X$ and ground field $\KK$ algebraically closed of characteristic zero.

\subsection{The Shape and Minimal Degrees of Multigraded Regularity}\label{subsec:shape-of-regularity}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

As the PI showed coordinate ring of a single point can have infinitely many minimal regular degrees \cite{bruceHellerSayrafi22}. On the Hirzebruch surface $\cH_2$ the module $S/I_p$ in Figure~\ref{fig:example-of-reg} exhibits precisely this behavior and its associated sheaf $\cO_p$ is regular in every degree. These corners are measured relative to $\Nef(X)$ and I propose that their infinitude reflects the choice of cone: the ambient nef cone can be too small to account for the directions of positivity available to $M$. A cone that sees the module should recover a form of persistence and finite generation, up to a uniform shift. The point example supports this expectation: its support-relative regularity has only one corner. This leads to three connected problems: determine the directions of regularity from support geometry, bound its minimal degrees using syzygies, and control the growth of its corners for powers of ideals.For a finitely generated graded $S$-module $M$, write $\cF= \widetilde{M}\neq0$ and $Z=\supp(\cF)$. Writing $D_\rho$ for the torus-invariant prime divisor associated to a ray $\rho$, set
\[
K_Z=\bigcap_{p\in Z}\cone\{[D_\rho]:p\notin D_\rho\},
\qquad
C_Z=\overline{\cone\{[C]:C\subset Z\text{ an integral curve}\}}^{\,\vee},
\]
with curve classes in $N_1(X)_{\RR}$. A rational class lies in $K_Z$ when some positive multiple has an ambient linear system with no base point on $Z$, and in $C_Z$ when its restriction to $Z$ is nef; thus $\Nef(X)_{\RR}\subset K_Z\subset C_Z$, where $K_Z$ sees only which torus orbits meet $Z$ and $C_Z$ sees the Mori geometry of $Z$, toric or not. I conjecture that they are the recession cones of $\reg(M)$ and $\reg(\cF)$.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:support-cone-duality}
If $H_B^0(M)=0$, there are $\aa_M,\bb_M,\aa_{\cF},\bb_{\cF}\in\Pic(X)$ such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\aa_M+(K_Z\cap\Pic(X))\subset\reg(M)\subset\bb_M+K_Z \quad \;\; \text{and} \quad \;\; 
\aa_{\cF}+(C_Z\cap\Pic(X))&\subset\reg(\cF)\subset\bb_{\cF}+C_Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{conjecture}

Module and sheaf regularity differ exactly by $H_B^1(M)$, the failure of sections to lift. In Figure~\ref{fig:example-of-reg}(c), $\reg(S/I_p)$ fills $K_p\supsetneq\Nef(X)_{\RR}$, while $C_p=\Pic(X)_{\RR}$. For the diagonal $\Delta\subset\PP^1\times\PP^1$, $K_\Delta$ is the nef cone but $C_\Delta$ is a half-plane: $\reg(S/(x_0y_1-x_1y_0))=\NN^2$, while $\reg(\cO_\Delta)=\{(a,b):a+b\ge0\}$, so one module corner shatters into infinitely many sheaf corners. I will approach the module bounds through toric modifications of linear systems whose base loci avoid $Z$. For sheaves, Fujita vanishing on $Z$ and the global generation forced by regularity point to the two inclusions, and comparing first syzygies along support curves offers a route to effective translates~\cite{keeler03,maclaganSmith04}.


For syzygies, our faithfulness theorem (Section~\ref{sec:prior-work}) gives finitely many corners, but no box, and when $K_Z\supsetneq\Nef(X)_{\RR}$, Conjecture~\ref{conj:support-cone-duality} forces infinitely many corners. So we measure corners relative $\Gamma_Z\coloneqq K_{X}\cap \Pic(X)$ instead. We define $\reg_Z(M)$ analogous to Maclagan--Smith regularity, but with $\Gamma_Z$ in place of $\Nef(X)$: $\dd\in\reg_Z(M)$ when $H_B^i(M)_{\dd-\mathbf g_1-\cdots-\mathbf g_{i-1}+\gamma}=0$ for all $i\ge1$ and $\gamma\in\Gamma_Z$, with each $\mathbf g_j$ among $B_1,\ldots,B_s$ and the minimal generators of $\Gamma_Z$. Then $\reg_Z(M)\subseteq\reg(M)$ is $\Gamma_Z$-stable. It equals $\reg(M)$ on products, and in Figure~\ref{fig:example-of-reg}(c), $\reg_Z(S/I_p)=\Gamma_p$ has one corner instead of infinitely many. I conjecture that, up to a uniform shift, regularity \emph{is} support-relative regularity, generated in a box determined by the Betti table.
 
\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:betti-boxes}
Fix $X$ and a basis of $\Pic(X)$. There are $\mathbf c_X\in\Nef(X)$ and, for each multigraded Betti table $\beta$, an explicit finite box $Q_X(\beta)$ such that every finitely generated graded $S$-module $M$ with $\beta^S(M)=\beta$, $\widetilde M\ne0$, and $H_B^0(M)=0$ satisfies
\[
\mathbf c_X+\reg(M)\;\subseteq\;\reg_Z(M)\;=\;\big(Q_X(\beta)\cap\reg_Z(M)\big)+\Gamma_Z.
\]
\end{conjecture}
 
The inclusion is a support-relative nef persistence, and the equality says the Betti table locates every $\Gamma_Z$-corner. Since $\Gamma_Z$ contains a translate of $K_Z\cap\Pic(X)$, together they imply the module half of Conjecture~\ref{conj:support-cone-duality}, with $\aa_M,\bb_M$ depending only on $\beta$. For the inclusion, general forms of each degree in $\Gamma_Z$ are nonzerodivisors on $M$, so the Maclagan--Smith argument~\cite{maclaganSmith04} should run by induction on $\dim Z$. It gives equality when $\dim Z=0$, and each further dimension should cost a bounded nef shift, which is uniform because $\Gamma_Z$ depends only on which torus orbits meet $Z$. The box is the heart of the matter. On products, where $\mathbf c_X=\zero$, ongoing work with Lauren Cranton Heller suggests $Q_X(\beta)=\prod_{j}[\ell_j(\beta),U_j(\beta)]$ with $\ell_j(\beta)=\min\{a_j:\beta_{0,\aa}\ne0\}$. We have proved this for $r=2$ and are extending it via projections and partial Koszul complexes. In general, persistence along faces of $\Gamma_Z$ and resolutions of the diagonal offer an approach~\cite{botbolChardin17,HHL24,brownSayrafi24}.
 
\begin{cor}\label{cor:finite-regularity-test}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:betti-boxes} and fix $\beta$. Only finitely many regions $\reg_Z(M)$ occur, each computable by finitely many tests in $Q_X(\beta)$, and $\reg_Z(M)\subseteq\reg(M)\subseteq\reg_Z(M)-\mathbf c_X$. 
\end{cor}
 
The same nonzerodivisor argument in \cite[Corollary~4.8]{maclaganSmith04} apploes to $\reg_Z$, with computable graded pieces~\cite{EMS00}. This would certify the entire support-relative region and hence the stated two-sided bounds for $\reg(M)$; on products, where $\mathbf c_X=\zero$, it computes $\reg(M)$ itself. Existing implementations, including ours~\cite{ABLS20}, inspect a fixed window without excluding generators outside it. On products, Theorem~\ref{thm:mgreg-main} expresses each test as ranks of Koszul strands; our box theorem makes the algorithm unconditional for $\PP^{n_1}\times\PP^{n_2}$. Our asymptotic theorem (Section~\ref{sec:prior-work}) bounds $\reg(I^p)$ between translates moving linearly in $p$, but permits corners to drift superlinearly along a face.
 
\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:linear-windows-powers}
Let $X=\PP^{n_1}\times\cdots\times\PP^{n_r}$, $I\subseteq S$ a homogeneous ideal, and $M$ a finitely generated graded module with $H_B^0(M)=0$. There are $A,C$ such that every corner $\vv$ of $\reg(I^pM)$ satisfies $\|\vv\|_\infty\le Ap+C$ whenever $\widetilde{I^pM}\ne0$.
\end{conjecture}
 
The Betti degrees of $I^pM$ grow linearly in $p$ via the Rees module, so this follows from Conjecture~\ref{conj:betti-boxes} if $U_j(\beta)$ is linear in the degrees of $\beta$. Since corners form an antichain, the window allows $O(p^{r-1})$ of them, and this is sharp: for $\mathfrak m$ generated by all Cox variables, $\reg(\mathfrak m^p)=\{\dd\in\NN^r:\sum_jd_j\ge p\}$ has $\binom{p+r-1}{r-1}$ corners. Picard rank alone would set the growth exponent.

 \subsection{A Gruson, Lazarsfeld, and Peskine Style Bound for Multigraded Regularity}
 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 A foundational result of Gruson, Lazarsfeld, and Peskine \cite{GLP83} states that if $C \subset \PP^r_{\KK}$ is an integral nondegenerate curve defined over an algebraically closed field $\KK$, then
\[
\reg\left(I_{C}\right) \leq \deg(C)-r+2
\]
where $I_{C} \subset \KK[x_0,\ldots,x_r]$ is the (saturated) homogeneous defining ideal of $C$. Since $\deg(C) = C \cdot H$ for the hyperplane class $H$, the theorem is a strong bridge between the intersection theory of $C$ and the degrees of its equations and syzygies. It sharpens classical work of Castelnuovo \cite{castelnuovo1893} on space curves and settles the curve case of the Eisenbud--Goto conjecture $\reg(I_C) \leq \deg(C) - \codim(C) + 1$ \cite{eisenbudGoto84}. It has inspired decades of work on optimal bounds \cite{pinkham86,lazarsfeld87,kwak98,kwak00} and on bounding regularity by the degrees of defining equations \cite{BEL91,einLazarsfeld93,CEL01}, and recent counterexamples to the general Eisenbud--Goto conjecture \cite{MP18,CCMPV19,McCulloughPeeva20} make such geometric bounds all the more valuable.  


%where $I_{C} \subset \KK[x_0,\ldots,x_r]$ is the (saturated) homogeneous defining ideal of $C$. It sharpens and completes classical work of Castelnuovo \cite{castelnuovo1893} on space curves, and it settles the curve case of the
%bound $\reg(I_C) \leq \deg(C) - \codim(C) + 1$ conjectured by Eisenbud and Goto \cite{eisenbudGoto84}. Beyond its original statement, \cite{GLP83} has inspired decades of work on geometric regularity bounds: finding optimal bounds \cite{pinkham86,lazarsfeld87,kwak98,kwak00}, and bounding regularity by the degrees of defining equations \cite{BEL91,einLazarsfeld93,CEL01}. Recent counterexamples to the Eisenbud--Goto conjecture in general \cite{MP18,CCMPV19,McCulloughPeeva20} make such genuinely geometric bounds all the more valuable. I propose to establish a multigraded analogue of the Gruson--Lazarsfeld--Peskine theorem for curves in smooth projective toric varieties.

 \begin{problem}\label{prob:GLP}
 	Prove a multigraded analogue of Gruson, Lazarsfeld, and Peskine's theorem for curves in smooth projective toric varieties.
\end{problem}

The objective is an explicit subset of $\reg(\cI_C)$ whose shape reflects the divisor relations of the ambient variety~\cite{maclaganSmith04}. Cobb has made progress toward Problem~\ref{prob:GLP} by adapting the kernel bundle techniques of Gruson, Lazarsfeld, and Peskine to products of projective spaces~\cite{cobb24}. The shape of my proposed answer comes from how the classical bound is proved. By definition, $\cI_C$ is $m$-regular if $H^q(\cI_C(m-q))=0$ for $q\geq1$, and the sequence $0\to\cI_C\to\cO_{\PP^r}\to\cO_C\to0$ splits this vanishing into three questions: (i) \emph{Ambient} ($q\geq3$): since $H^{q-1}(C,\cO_{C}(m-q))=0$ for $q\geq3$, we get $H^q(\cI_{C}(m-q)) \cong H^q(\cO_{\PP^r}(m-q))$, which vanishes at these twists since $\PP^r$ has no intermediate cohomology. (ii) \emph{Speciality} ($q=2$): $H^2(\cI_C(m-2))\cong H^1(\cO_C(m-2))$ vanishes when $\cO_{C}(m-2)$ is nonspecial, e.g., when $(m-2)\deg(C)>2p_a(C)-2$; at $m=\deg(C)-r+2$ this follows from Castelnuovo's genus bound. (iii) \emph{Interpolation} ($q=1$): $H^1(\cI_{C}(m-1))$ is the cokernel of restriction $H^0(\cO_{\PP^r}(m-1))\to H^0(\cO_C(m-1))$, and vanishes if and only if every section on $C$ extends to $\PP^r$. This surjectivity is the heart of the theorem and is proved by kernel bundle arguments. 

The constant $\deg(C)-r+2$ also has a geometric explanation: nondegeneracy forces every secant line $\ell$ to meet $C$ in length at most $\deg(C)-r+2$, and interpolation along $\ell$ shows that an $s$-secant line forces $\reg(\cI_{C})\geq s$. Hence the bound is governed by maximal secant lines. 

Now let $X$ be as in this section with $n\geq2$. Say that an integral curve $C\subset X$ is \emph{nondegenerate} if each restriction $H^0(X,B_j)\to H^0(C,B_j|_C)$ is injective. Set $r_j\coloneqq\dim_\KK H^0(X,B_j)-1$, $d_j\coloneqq\langle B_j\cdot C\rangle$, and the \emph{defect} $\delta_j\coloneqq d_j-r_j$. Maclagan--Smith regularity of $\cI_C$ at $\dd$ tests the twists $\dd-B_i$, then $\dd-B_i-B_j$, and so on, so the same three questions arise. I propose answering each by a region depending only on the numerical class of $C$ and on $X$:
\begin{enumerate}[wide, labelwidth=!, labelindent=0pt]

\item \emph{Ambient vanishing.} Membership in $\reg(\cO_X)$ gives $H^q(X,\cO_X(\dd-\sum_jv_jB_j))=0$ for $q>0$ and $|\vv|=q$. Since $C$ is a curve, for $q\geq3$ these are exactly the ideal-sheaf tests.

\item \emph{Nonspeciality.} Choose an explicit numerical bound $\widehat G_X([C])\geq p_a(C)$. For example, $A=\sum_jB_j$ is very ample, and multiplication of the restricted section spaces shows that $C$ spans at least a $\PP^R$, where $R=\sum_jr_j$. Thus one may take Castelnuovo's bound $\pi(D,R)$, where $D=\sum_jd_j$: writing $D-1=m(R-1)+\epsilon$ with $0\leq\epsilon<R-1$, this is $\binom{m}{2}(R-1)+m\epsilon$. On a surface, adjunction gives the exact genus and can be used instead. Every second twist is nonspecial on
\[
\cH_X([C])\coloneqq
\left\{\dd\in\Pic(X)\ \middle|\
\dd\cdot C\geq2\widehat G_X([C])-1+2\max\{d_j\}\right\},
\]
because $\deg\cO_C(\dd-B_i-B_j)>2p_a(C)-2$.

\item \emph{Interpolation.} Guided by the secant-line explanation above, set $M_X\coloneqq\overline{\operatorname{NE}}(X)\cap N_1(X)_{\ZZ}$ and $e_j(\gamma)\coloneqq B_j\cdot\gamma$. Call $0\neq\gamma\in M_X$ \emph{eligible} if $e_j(\gamma)<r_j$ for some $j$, and put
\[
q_C(\gamma)\coloneqq\min_{j:\,e_j(\gamma)<r_j}
\bigl(\delta_j+e_j(\gamma)\bigr),
\qquad w(\gamma)\coloneqq\max_je_j(\gamma).
\]
If an integral rational curve $C'\neq C$ has eligible class $\gamma$, its image under $B_j$ spans at most $\PP^{e_j(\gamma)}$. The resulting sections vanishing on $C'$, together with nondegeneracy of $C$, give $\length(C\cap C')\leq q_C(\gamma)+1$. When $C'$ is smooth, arbitrary values on a subscheme of length $q_C(\gamma)+1$ can be interpolated by a line bundle of degree at least $q_C(\gamma)$. Requiring this degree at every first twist motivates:
\[
\cS_X([C])\coloneqq
\left\{\dd\in\Pic(X)\ \middle|\
\dd\cdot\gamma\geq q_C(\gamma)+w(\gamma)
\text{ for every eligible }\gamma\in M_X\right\}.
\]
The definition tests all eligible lattice classes, including nonprimitive ones; the claim that these numerical inequalities suffice for interpolation is conjectural.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:multigraded-glp}
If $C\subset X$ is an integral nondegenerate curve, then
\[
\cR_X([C])\coloneqq\cS_X([C])\cap\cH_X([C])\cap\reg(\cO_X)\ \subseteq\ \reg(\cI_C).
\]
\end{conjecture}

On $\PP^r$, the line class gives $m\geq\deg(C)-r+2$; the higher eligible multiples give weaker inequalities, and Castelnuovo's genus bound supplies nonspeciality at the same threshold. Thus the conjecture recovers the classical theorem. The proposed bound can improve on Cobb's bound even for a small, geometrically familiar curve: the image $C\subset X=\PP^2\times\PP^2$ of $[u:v]\mapsto\bigl([u^3:u^2v:v^3],[u^2:uv:v^2]\bigr)$. The inequalities defining $\cS_X([C])$ reduce to $a\geq1$ and $b\geq2$, while the genus estimate $\widehat G_X([C])=\pi(5,4)=1$ gives the redundant condition $3a+2b\geq7$. In fact, $\cR_X([C])=\reg(\cI_C)=(1,2)+\NN^2$, whereas Cobb's bound gives $(3,3)+\NN^2$ \cite[Proposition~B]{cobb24}. The remaining condition is precise: for every $\dd\in\cR_X([C])$ and each $i$, prove that $H^0(X,\cO_X(\dd-B_i))\to H^0(C,\cO_C(\dd-B_i))$ is surjective.  \subsection{Multigraded Regularity of Torus-Invariant Subvarieties}
 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 
On a smooth projective toric variety $X = X_\Sigma$, the torus-invariant irreducible subvarieties are in dimension-reversing bijection with the cones of $\Sigma$. If $S=\KK[x_\rho \mid \rho\in \Sigma(1)]$ is the Cox ring, the subvariety $Z_\sigma$ corresponding to $\sigma\in\Sigma$ has Cox ideal $I_{Z_\sigma} = \langle x_\rho \mid \rho \in \sigma(1) \rangle$, which is resolved by a multigraded Koszul complex. Even so, the multigraded regularity of these subvarieties can be subtle: the four torus-fixed points of the Hirzebruch surface $\cH_2$ exhibit two distinct regularities, one with infinitely many minimal corners~\cite{bruceHellerSayrafi22}. These highly structured subvarieties are thus a natural testing ground for how regularity reflects positivity beyond syzygies, yet before the work below no concrete description of their regularity was known, even for torus-fixed points.

\begin{problem}\label{prob:torus-invariant-mg}
	Let $Z_{\sigma} \subset X$ be an irreducible torus-invariant subvariety corresponding to the cone $\sigma$. Give an explicit combinatorial characterization of $\reg(I_{Z_\sigma})$ and $\reg(S/I_{Z_\sigma})$. 
\end{problem}

We ask for both because, unlike in the standard graded case, $\reg(S)$ need not have a single corner, so the two can behave quite differently \cite{bruceHellerSayrafi22}.

Our approach combines the combinatorics of Hilbert functions with explicit higher cohomology of line bundles restricted to torus-invariant subvarieties. With my graduate student Jacob Lehman Duke, I have settled torus-fixed points. For a maximal cone $\sigma$, the variables surviving in $S/I_{Z_\sigma}$ are those indexed by rays outside $\sigma$, so the degrees of monomials not vanishing at $Z_\sigma$ form the monoid $Q_\sigma=\{\sum_{\rho\notin\sigma(1)}a_\rho[D_\rho]\mid a_\rho\in\NN\}\subset\Pic(X)$. By smoothness, the classes $[D_\rho]$ for $\rho\notin\sigma(1)$ form a lattice basis of $\Pic(X)$, so the Hilbert function of $S/I_{Z_\sigma}$ is $1$ on $Q_\sigma$ and $0$ elsewhere.

\begin{prop}
	If $Z_{\sigma} \in X$ is a torus-fixed point corresponding to a maximal cone $\sigma \in \Sigma$, then 
	\[
		\reg\left(S/I_{Z_\sigma}\right) = Q_{\sigma} \quad \quad \text{and} \quad \quad  \reg\left(I_{Z_\sigma}\right) = \reg\left(S \right) \cap \bigcap_{j=1}^{s} \left(B_{j}+Q_{\sigma}\right).
	\]
\end{prop}

A cohomology argument then reduces $\reg(I_{Z_\sigma})$ to surjectivity of evaluation at each first twist, i.e.\ $\dd-B_j\in Q_\sigma$ for all $j$, giving the intersection. Notably, $\reg(S/I_{Z_\sigma})$ depends only on the rays of $\Sigma$ and on $\sigma$, so it is unchanged across toric models sharing these data. Since $Z_\sigma$ is toric, with fan given by the images of the cones containing $\sigma$ modulo $\sigma$, we plan to compute these vanishing regions inductively via toric \v{C}ech complexes.

\section{Uniform Homological Algebra: Regularity on the Cox Category}
\label{sec:uniform-homological-algebra-on-toric}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

This part of the proposal is deliberately speculative: \emph{multigraded regularity may be asking the right questions, but in the wrong category.} Work of the PI and others, exposes the fragility of the classical link between cohomological vanishing and syzygies~\cite{maclaganSmith04,bruceHellerSayrafi26,bruceHellerSayrafi22}. Even the Cox ring of the Hirzebruch surface $\cH_2$, as a free module over itself, is not $\zero$-regular~\cite[Example~4.3]{maclaganSmith04}. I propose to test whether treating birational models together provides a more natural setting.

Uniformity across toric birational models is a growing theme in multigraded algebra, beginning with Hanlon--Hicks--Lazarev's diagonal, which depends only on the fan rays~\cite{HHL24}. Work on short resolutions, cellular constructions, and derived categories has developed this theme~\cite{brownErman24-1,faveroHuang23,faveroHuang25,faveroSapronov24,CH25,borisovHan25,berkeschHellerSmithYang25,han26}. Ballard et al. give a striking reformulation of King's conjecture: a full strong exceptional collection of line bundles can fail to exist on an individual smooth projective toric variety, yet exists in the \emph{Cox category} incorporating its birational models~\cite{longLiveKing}. I propose to use its uniform diagonal to "repair" classical regularity and make the structure sheaf $\zero$-regular on every smooth projective model.

\subsection{The Cox Category and the Diagonal}\label{subsec:cox-diagonal}

Let $X$ be a projective normal toric variety over an algebraically closed field $\KK$ of characteristic zero, with Cox ring $S$. Varying the irrelevant ideal gives models $X_i$ indexed by maximal GKZ chambers and associated smooth toric stacks $\cX_i$, with $\cX_i=X_i$ when $X_i$ is smooth. Let $D^b(-) \coloneqq D^{b}(\Coh(-))$ and all functor that follow be derived. Following Ballard et al. and choose a common smooth toric stack with proper birational maps $\pi_i\colon\widetilde{\cX}\to\cX_i$ and set $D_{\Cox}(X) \coloneqq \langle\pi_i^*D^b(\cX_i)\rangle \subset D^b(\widetilde{\cX})$. This category is independent of the choice and contains each $D^b(\cX_i)$ as a full subcategory. The Cox grading determines a half-open zonotope $Q\colon\ZZ^{\Sigma(1)}\longrightarrow\Cl(X)$ and  $Z_X\coloneqq Q_{\RR}\left((-1,0]^{\Sigma(1)}\right)$. The classes mapping into $Z_X$ form the finite \emph{Bondal--Thomsen collection} $\Theta\subset\Cl(X)$. For $\alpha\in\Theta$, pull back its line bundle from a chamber containing $-\alpha$. The resulting objects $\cO_{\Cox}(\alpha)$ are independent of this choice and form a full strong exceptional collection~\cite{longLiveKing}.

 =\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta\in\Theta}
 \bigl(S(\alpha)\boxtimes S(\beta)\bigr)^{m_{p,\alpha,\beta}}$ for $p\geq 0$,

 h(\alpha)\coloneqq\min\{p:m_{p,\alpha,\beta}\ne0\text{ for some }\beta\},
 \qquad \nu(\alpha)\coloneqq\max\{1,h(\alpha)\}.

\begin{problem}\label{prob:cox-heights}
Describe $h(\alpha)$ for every $\alpha\in\Theta_H$ combinatorially from the divisor-class map, zonotope, and GKZ fan, without constructing and minimizing the entire diagonal complex. 
\end{problem}

\begin{defn}[Proposed Cox regularity]\label{def:cox-regularity}
An object $\cE\in D_{\Cox}(X)$ is \emph{$\zero$-regular} if
\[
 \Ext^{q}_{D_{\Cox}(X)}
 \left(\cO_{\Cox}(\alpha)^\vee,\cE\right)=0
\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Theta_H\text{ and }q\geq\nu(\alpha).
\]
\end{defn}
For a coherent sheaf $\cF$ on $\cX_i$, the $\Theta$-Transform Lemma \cite[Lem. 1.6]{longLiveKing} and projection formula give
\[
 \Ext^{q}
 \left(\cO_{\Cox}(\alpha)^\vee,
       \pi_i^*(\cF(\dd))\right)
 \cong H^q(\cX_i,\cF(\dd+\alpha)).
\]
Let $\cR_i(\cF)$ be the classes $\dd\in\Pic(\cX_i)$ satisfying these vanishings. Bondal--Thomsen vanishing makes $\cO_{\Cox}$ $\zero$-regular and gives $\Nef(\cX_i)\cap\Pic(\cX_i)\subseteq\cR_i(\cO_{\cX_i})$.

The cutoff reflects diagonal reconstruction: degree-$q$ cohomology in diagonal degree $p$ contributes in position $p-q$. Requiring $q<h(\alpha)$ at positive height leaves only positive positions; setting $\nu=1$ at height zero allows global sections. On $\PP^n$, $h(-j)=j$ for $0\leq j\leq n$, recovering classical $\zero$-regularity. On $\cH_2$, Figure~\ref{fig:h2-zonotopes} and direct cohomology calculations give $\cR_{\cH_2}(\cO)=\Nef(\cH_2)\cap\Pic(\cH_2)$. More generally, we have the following conditional relationship Maclagan-Smith regularity.


\begin{prop}\label{prop:ms-compare}
Let $\ell(\alpha)\coloneqq\min\{|\vv|:\alpha\in-\sum_jv_jB_j+\Nef(X_i)\}$. If $\ell(\alpha)\le\nu(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha\in\Theta$, then $\reg(\cF)\subseteq\cR_i(\cF)$ for all coherent $\cF$ on $X_i$.
\end{prop}
On $\cH_2$ the hypothesis holds, and $\reg(\cO)=\Nef\smallsetminus\{\zero\}\subsetneq\cR_{\cH_2}(\cO)=\Nef$: Maclagan--Smith tests $H^2(\cO(K))\neq0$ because $K=-2h$ is minus a sum of two nef generators, while $K\notin\Theta$ (it is the excluded vertex of $Z_X$). The cutoff must also be tested against the weighted theory. On $\PP(1,2)$, $\Theta=\{0,-1,-2\}$ has heights $0,0,1$, so $\cR(\cO)=\ZZ_{\ge0}$, whereas the Maclagan--Smith and Benson regularities are $\ZZ_{\ge-1}$ \cite{benson04}. The Serre-symmetrized cutoff $\nu^S(\alpha)=\max\{1,h(\alpha),\dim X+1-h(K_X-\alpha)\}$ (last term omitted if $K_X-\alpha\notin\Theta$) agrees with $\nu$ on $\PP^n$ and products, keeps $\cR_{\cH_2}(\cO)=\Nef$, and recovers $\ZZ_{\ge-1}$ on $\PP(1,2)$. Determining the cutoffs with $\delta(X)=0$ is part of Question~\ref{q:cox-regularity-tests}.


\begin{question}\label{q:cox-regularity-tests}
For a smooth projective model $X_i$ and coherent $\cF$, does the proposed definition satisfy (1) $\cR_i(\cF)+\Nef(X_i)_{\ZZ}\subseteq\cR_i(\cF)$, and (2)~\emph{global generation} of $\cF(\dd)$ for all $\dd\in\cR_i(\cF)$?
\end{question}

Nef translates of test line bundles can leave $Z_X$. I will seek resolutions using the original labels with controlled heights:
\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:cox-nef-resolutions}
Let $X_i$ be a smooth projective chamber, and let $G_i$ minimally generate its integral nef monoid. For every $\alpha\in\Theta_H$ and $L\in G_i$, there is an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow\cC_N\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow\cC_0
 \longrightarrow\cO_{X_i}(L+\alpha)\longrightarrow0,
\]
where $\cC_j$ is a finite sum of line bundles $\cO_{X_i}(\beta)$ with $\beta\in\Theta_H$ and $\nu(\beta)\leq\nu(\alpha)+j$.
\end{conjecture}

For $\dd\in\cR_i(\cF)$, tensoring by $\cF(\dd)$ gives cohomology terms $H^{q+j}(\cF(\dd+\beta))$, which vanish for $q\geq\nu(\alpha)$ by the height bound; iteration would prove persistence. I will begin with toric Koszul complexes on products and Hirzebruch surfaces, then study GKZ wall crossing. For global generation, I will analyze degree-zero terms in diagonal reconstruction and their evaluation maps.

Finally, Cranton Heller's ceiling truncations and their HHL Fourier--Mukai description give complexes in the nef range that can retain higher module homology under arbitrarily ample twists~\cite{CH25}. I will study how this homology affects the comparison with Maclagan--Smith regularity, distinguishing homological behavior shared by the models from each model's additional vanishing.


 Every $\cE\in D_{\Cox}(X)$ has a unique minimal model $C_\bullet(\cE)$ with $C_p(\cE)=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Theta}\cO_{\Cox}(\beta)^{b_{p,\beta}(\cE)}$, the \emph{Cox Betti numbers} of $\cE$. Put $\kappa(\beta)\coloneqq\min\{p-\nu(\alpha)+1 : m_{p,\alpha,\beta}\ne0\}$, call $\cE$ \emph{Cox-quasilinear} if $p\ge\kappa(\beta)$ whenever $b_{p,\beta}(\cE)\ne0$, and let the \emph{defect} $\delta(X)\ge0$ be the least integer with $\Ext^q(\cO_{\Cox}(\alpha)^\vee,\cO_{\Cox}(\beta))=0$ for all $q\geq\nu(\alpha)+\kappa(\beta)+\delta(X)$.
\begin{goalTheorem}[Cox Eisenbud--Goto]\label{gt:cox-eg}
Let $\cE\in D_{\Cox}(X)$. (1) If $\cE$ is $\zero$-regular, it is Cox-quasilinear. (2) If $\cE$ is Cox-quasilinear, then $\Ext^q(\cO_{\Cox}(\alpha)^\vee,\cE)=0$ for all $\alpha\in\Theta_H$ and $q\ge\nu(\alpha)+\delta(X)$. (3) $\delta(X)=0$ for products of projective spaces, where (1)--(2) recover Theorem~\ref{thm:mgreg-main}.
\end{goalTheorem}
Part (1) follows from the diagonal spectral sequence, and (2) is formal. The defect is genuine: on $\cH_2$, $\cO_{D_4}$ has minimal model $\cO(-h)\to\cO$ and is Cox-quasilinear, yet $H^1(\cO_{D_4}(-h))=H^1(\cO_{\PP^1}(-2))\ne0$. The obstructing pairs satisfy $\alpha+\beta=K_{\cH_2}$, the same Serre term that breaks Maclagan--Smith regularity (Proposition~\ref{prop:ms-compare}). \emph{Milestone:} (1) in general and (3) for $\PP^n$ and $\PP^1\times\PP^1$. \emph{Fallback:} (1)--(2) are already an effective regularity test with a computable defect.

\section{Extending Computational Algebra Software}\label{sec:comp}

Computation drives much of the recent progress in multigraded commutative algebra and toric geometry, and the PI has a track record of building such tools. Each project below is targeted and produces a concrete community tool that also supports this proposal.

  %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 \subsection{The \texttt{toricStacks} Package for \textit{Macaulay2}}
  %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The lack of software is a major bottleneck for homological invariants of stacks. A general framework is out of reach, but toric stacks are tractable: they correspond to \emph{stacky fans}, whose well-developed combinatorics makes computation feasible \cite{toricStacks1,toricStacks2,borisovChenSmith05}, but no implementation exists. Given the success of \textit{NormalToricVarieties}, a \textit{toricStacks} package would benefit the community and directly support the experiments in \S\ref{sec:syzygies-on-stacks}.

\begin{problem}\label{prob:toric-stacks-m2}
Develop a robust \textit{Macaulay2} package for computations with toric stacks.
\end{problem}

The package will (i) construct toric stacks from stacky fans and from families such as weighted projective stacks and root stacks, (ii) compute the Cox ring with its $\Cl(\cX)$-grading and irrelevant ideal, and (iii) support coherent sheaves, including sheaf cohomology via local cohomology \cite{EMS00}. This is joint work in progress with Maya Banks and John Cobb; (i) is complete and (ii) is underway.

  %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
 \subsection{A Database of Human-Generated Examples in Algebra}
  %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
Good examples drive conjectures, theorems, and algorithms, and a trove of human-generated examples sits unused in the documentation and tests of open-source computer algebra systems. \textit{Macaulay2} and its roughly 250 packages contain thousands of rings, ideals, and modules, nearly all chosen by experts to illustrate something interesting. Yet they are scattered across source files, not easily accessible.
\begin{problem}\label{prob:database-m2}
	Build a \textit{Macaulay2} package collecting the algebraic objects in its source and documentation into a queryable database with provenance and invariants.
\end{problem}

The package will parse the \texttt{EXAMPLE} and \texttt{TEST} blocks of installed packages, re-execute them in a sandbox, and record each resulting object as directly callable, with its source location and authorship. Much of the parsing already exists; the work lies in execution and curation. The result would be a testbed for conjectures and a benchmark suite easing future \textit{Macaulay2} development. As calls grow for AI and machine learning tools built by mathematicians on mathematician-owned data, this is a small first step, with all authorship tracked and acknowledged.
